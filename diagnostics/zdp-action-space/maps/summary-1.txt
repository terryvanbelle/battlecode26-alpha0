==================== map-baby-move | Decision point 1 (BABY_MOVE) in zdp (java/bots/zdp). Covered: the code path from open through mask, 

## 1 BABY_MOVE (72 options) kind: Mixed. Options 0-38 are branches of zdb's moveDecision cascade, numbered in its priority order (D.java:52-59). Each branch chooses its own target, applies zdb's thresholds, and drives Nav, which keeps state across turns. Options 39-71 are primitive actions: step, turn, hold, strafe and risky step (D.java:61-66). They were added so the net can make any move, and zdb never labels them.
-- fallback_explanation: How a fallback happens: netMove runs the chosen option's executor. If the executor returns false or throws, zdb's moveDecision runs in the same turn, and the label becomes 0x8000|(k<<6)|zdb_label (Baby.java:2193-2207). D.fallback turns that bit into the record's fallback flag (D.java:158-162).

Size: 0.43% of all 622,495 picks (~2,676). The rate is low because the net is a saturated copy of the cascade: its mean top-choice probability is 0.9928. Its top choice is usually the branch zdb would take in that state, and that branch's conditions hold.

Fallback concentrates on deviations: 13.0% of deviations fall back, against 0.28% of top choices. The deviation rate is 1.19% and the real (non-fallback) deviation rate 1.04%. Most deviations come from the 0.005 floor.

Cause: for several options the mask is looser than the executor's own preconditions, and no feature exposes those preconditions.
- FLANK: the mask lacks 'enemy baby within dist2 8' and 'an adjacent tile it cannot see'; enemy facing is not a feature.
- WAR: the mask is any enemy, enemy king, threatened king or alert, while war() is gated by role, raw cheese, hunting and distance.
- AIM_CARRY: the mask lacks 'target within Chebyshev 3' and 'free flight tile'.
- RETREAT: the mask lacks hp <= 40 and Chebyshev > 2.
- YIELD_KING and AMMO_AWAY: the mask lacks almost every condition, so they fall back 100% of the time.
- FORM_KING: distance and crowd checks.
- ATTACK: noKing exhausted.
- SEEK and MINE_EXPLORE: success depends on random samples of unseen home tiles.
- FLEE_CAT: all away directions blocked.

By source: WAR ~1,108 (~786 at the argmax), FLANK ~496 (70% of its deviations and ~60% of its argmax picks), AIM_CARRY ~403, SEEK ~343, RETREAT ~128, ATTACK ~99, FORM_KING ~50, FLEE_CAT ~34. Primitives never fall back, because their masks (2298-2305) match their executor guards (2437-2460).

Consequences:
1. rl_train keeps fallback records and gives the credit to the chosen k; the flag is only a diagnostic (rl_train.py:525-542, flag read at 538). This is valid for the MDP, since choosing k in that state does produce zdb's move. But k's effect then switches between its own behaviour and zdb's on a condition the net cannot see, so the value learned for k mixes two behaviours.
2. Some executors change state before failing, so the fallback is not exactly what zdb would have done. seek() clears mineTarget and seekUntil (1877, 1881). exploreHome() draws from G.rand (1816-1817). flank and yieldKing can turn before a failed step (Nav.java:119-126).
3. For FLANK and AIM_CARRY, most of the exploration the floor spends on that option ends up running zdb's move instead.

Point 1 is not trainable in any RL or ES config (configs/es-*.json and rl-*.json train [5,7,10,11,12,13,16], [5,7,10,11,12,13] or [11]). Today, therefore, this only affects the data the other points learn from.
-- context_dependence: What an option does depends on much more than its index.

1. Role. war() branches on GATHER, FIGHT, hunting and raw cheese (1249-1268). pressKing gates on GATHER and raw (1169-1171). The mine block ticks only for GATHER/FIGHT (2473-2480). STALL_CAT's mask requires ATTACK in coop (2276). The cascade limits FIGHT_CAT, KNOWN_CAT and RAID_FIGHT to FIGHT (1040, 1059-1062), ATTACK to the ATTACK role (1035), SEEK to GATHER (1075) and SCOUT to scouts (1064). Most of these options are offered to every role, so a pick can run code in a role zdb never ran it in: ATTACK for a gatherer, the mine block for an attacker, SCOUT for a fighter.

2. Coop. nearestEnemy is null in coop (92-100), so the enemy options mostly drop out of the mask. WAR stays offered through the enemy king or an alert (2278). The cascade also requires !coop for AIM_CARRY, RETREAT, RAID, FLANK, FACE and WAR (1018-1037).

3. Cascade order. Each zdb label was produced only after every earlier branch failed, so the net learned option k only inside that region. Picked outside it, k's executor runs in states zdb never ran it in:
   - WAR with an enemy within dist2 8, where zdb takes FACE for non-gatherers (1033 comes before 1037);
   - DELIVER with 1 raw cheese, where the cascade needs 30, or 10 with no cheese target, or late game (1044);
   - CAT_DEFEND or END_CAT away from any king or before round 1850 (1008, 1010-1012).

4. Errand state the features see only in part: Nav.target, bugging, stall and bestDist; exploreTarget; mineTarget, mineShift and the idle counter; the seek window; raidTarget and raidSince; lastEKing and noKing; cheeseTarget; catHunt. The same index either continues an errand or starts a new one depending on this state. Choosing another target for one turn wipes Nav's progress toward the old one (Nav.java:74-79).

5. States only the net creates. The raider feature (D.java:275) was always 0 in zdb data, because only roleSet sets it (1911-1915). train_dp gives a constant column unit scale (train_dp.py:120-121), so its first-layer weights receive no gradient and stay at their random values. A net-made raider (R_RAIDER was picked 8,043 times at point 5 in cpi-x0) therefore shifts the BABY_MOVE logits by untrained weights. RAID and RAID_FIGHT are picked only through the floor, so the cascade's raider branches (1027, 1062) run for such a rat only on fallback turns.
-- features: The input has 76 values: 60 situation features (D.java:247-323) and a 16-value comms block (D.java:324, 688-702).

Included: role, raw cheese, bank, coop flag, round, hp; cat present and its catDist4; nearest enemy present and its dist2; carry state; Chebyshev distance to cheeseTarget, mineTarget, nearest king, home and lastEKing (plus its age); counts of kings, mines, own mines, visible foes, friends and foe kings; flags for raider, scout, eKing, action ready, turning ready, cash site, seek window, threatened king, form site, idleDue, catAtKing, atMine, crowd and unreported news; Nav.stall; absolute x, y, W, H; facing; and canMove and avoid each packed into one 8-bit integer (303-306).

The point-1 net used in RL is the pass-2 imitation net: net-v2's point 1 is 60-64-64-39 (runs/dp/net-v2/net.json, checked locally). Widening adds input columns with zero weights and new output rows with zero weights and bias -20 (docs/decision-points.md:189-190; scripts/widen_dpnet.py:8-14; configs/rl-m2.json:3). Point 1 is never trained, so the comms block is invisible to BABY_MOVE and the primitives cannot depend on the state. I did not read v39's weights; this follows from how widening is documented.

Missing for a policy that does anything other than copy zdb:
- the direction or offset to any target (only Chebyshev distances), so the net cannot tell where a target lies relative to its facing or to the enemy;
- enemy facing, hp and positions beyond the nearest enemy's dist2 (FLANK and the war standoff depend on these);
- ally positions, and terrain (walls or dirt) around the rat;
- cat facing and lane;
- the current Nav, explore or raid target and its age, and the previous option;
- the mask itself, so the net cannot tell which options can act;
- flight tiles for throws, and bytecodes left.

The absolute coordinates are not mirror-invariant. The packed canMove and avoid integers force the MLP to decode bits from a single scalar.

-- generalization_proposal: Diagnosis: options 0-38 are zdb's errands, each with zdb's target choice and thresholds built into its executor. From the zdb copy, the net can reach only a few kinds of alternative: another errand outside the context it was designed for (WAR at close range, DELIVER with 1 cheese, ATTACK for a gatherer), a duplicate (1/22/2/27, 24/29, 12/28), a fallback to zdb, or a one-step primitive move whose logit does not depend on the state. The alternatives a better player would want cannot be expressed at all: another mine, another king, intercepting an enemy carrier, holding a spot. This fits the 'every direction is downhill' landscape. I propose two stages.

Stage 1: cleanup that any redesign needs. It also makes today's list trainable.
(a) Make the mask equal the executor. Write side-effect-free can-act checks for:
   - FLANK: an enemy baby within dist2 8 with an unseen, free, non-avoided adjacent tile;
   - WAR: per branch;
   - AIM_CARRY: a dry run of the step x target search;
   - RETREAT: hp <= 40 and Chebyshev > 2;
   - YIELD_KING, AMMO_AWAY, FORM_KING;
   - ATTACK: rushTarget non-null, without its reset;
   - SEEK and MINE_EXPLORE: draw the exploreHome sample before the mask from a separate RNG and cache it.
   After that a fallback cannot happen, and the 0x8000 path and the fallback flag can be deleted.
(b) Delete 0, 6, 9, 17 and 21. Merge 22 into 1, 29 into 24, 28 into 12, 2 and 27 into one GO_CAT, and 35 into P_HOLD.
(c) Remove role from the executors. Split war() into WAR_KING (goTo the enemy king), ENGAGE (goTo the nearest enemy), STANDOFF (face the nearest enemy) and ALERT (goTo the alert location). Drop the role and raw gates in DELIVER and PRESS. Role stays a feature.
(d) Move hidden bookkeeping out of the options and into netBook: the idle tick, refreshMine, stallTick and lastEKing. seek() and exploreHome() must not change state when they fail.
(e) Features: unpack canMove and avoid into 16 bits; add side-frame (dx, dy) to cheese, mine, king, nearest enemy, enemy king, cat, alert, form site, cash site and the current Nav target; add the mask bits as inputs; and zero the first-layer columns of features that were constant in zdb data (raider).
(f) Take the primitives out of BABY_MOVE (see the micro head below) or give them per-direction features. As they are now (zero weights, bias -20, no direction inputs), they only add floor noise.

Stage 2: the generalization. Replace the errands with a target pointer plus a micro head.

Target head. It opens on every movement-ready turn, as now. Candidates are typed concrete locations, taken from generators that already exist:
- our kings (Comms.kings);
- cheeseTarget plus up to 3 visible cheese tiles;
- pickMine's ranked list (1473-1502), top 4;
- pickRaid's enemy-half mines (1753-1768);
- the enemy king, lastEKing, and up to 3 rush spots (1204-1220);
- up to 3 visible enemy babies;
- the alert location, formSite and cashSite;
- the visible or known cat;
- 1-2 explore samples from a separate RNG;
- CONTINUE (the current Nav target) and HOLD.

Each candidate gets features: a type one-hot, side-frame dx/dy, Chebyshev distance, is-current-target, crowding (allies within 4), enemy threat near it, and type-specific attributes (cheese amount, kingHeld, enemy hp, whether the enemy sees us, age of the information). Each type has a mode: approach (Nav.goTo), reach (stand in transfer or adjacent range, as deliver and approachCat do), wander, flee or face. The mask is simply 'the candidate exists', so every pick executes and there is no fallback.

A cheap way to build it with today's pipeline: MINE_PICK already chooses among ranked candidates through fixed slots with per-slot features in a flat input (D.java:99-100, 822-852). The target head can use K slots in a fixed type order (K of about 24) without changing DNet or the record format. A shared per-candidate scorer would generalize across slots better, but it needs DNet and Python changes, so it can come later.

Micro head. A new point that opens only in contact states: an enemy baby within dist2 8, carrying an enemy, or a cat lane within reach. Its options are 8 steps, 8 turns, hold and strafes. Each direction gets features: free, avoided, trap risk, seen by each nearby enemy, number of adjacent enemies, change in distance to the chosen target, and the throw-line value when carrying. zdb's FLANK, FACE, the war standoff, the move and turn part of AIM_CARRY (the throw stays an act), YIELD_KING, AMMO_AWAY, FLEE_CAT and the retreat step become labels here. The primitives that make the net capability-complete then live where their logits can depend on the state.

Imitation. Instrument moveDecision so each branch reports the (type, location, mode) it acted on (the goTo, flee, face or wander argument) and the step it took. zdb's own exploreTarget, mineTarget, raidTarget and cheeseTarget are always inserted as candidates, so the label always exists. Exact zdb replay is given up. The check becomes the win rate of the new imitation start against zdb and against the field, with about 40% against the field as the competence bar.

RL. Advantages are the team outcome minus a team-state baseline over 10-round bins (rl_train.py:25, 35-39). A single per-turn movement choice out of roughly 1,200 per team per game is almost invisible in that signal. Either re-open the target head only when the target is reached or invalid, on interrupt events, or every N turns, or rely on CONTINUE with is-current features so that a change of target is an explicit decision.

Why this generalizes: targets zdb never picks become first-class choices; an option's meaning no longer depends on role or cascade order; every offered action executes; and every alternative is described by features, so learning about one candidate transfers to similar ones.
-- proposal_cost: Stage 1: about 200-300 Java lines (can-act checks, the war split and the bookkeeping move in Baby.java; feature changes in D.java). Option-table changes in scripts/dp_points.py and decisions.py. New zdb imitation data and a train_dp run for point 1, then a 128-256-game check against zdb. About 1-2 days.

Stage 2 with fixed slots: about 400-600 Java lines, split roughly as candidate generators and slot features ~200, executor modes ~80, the micro point (open, mask, executor, features) ~150, label instrumentation in moveDecision ~60 and commitment ~40. About 100 Python lines (new point registration and label decoding), plus the data collection, imitation and check runs. About 3-5 days. A shared per-candidate scorer would add about 300-400 Python lines (variable-length candidate records through rl_data, dpnet_io, train_dp, rl_train and build_dpnet) and about 60 Java lines in DNet.

Risks:
- Imitation fidelity of the target head: zdb's priority logic has to be recoverable from the candidate features. Measure per-type label accuracy before RL.
- Input size: slots enlarge the input. Bytecodes are not limited now; tournament limits would need a separate budget pass.
- Nav state: switching targets resets the bug and flow state (Nav.java:74-79), so CONTINUE or commitment is needed to avoid thrashing.
- G.rand use changes, so there is no bit-exact zdb replay. The user has allowed this.

-- cross_cutting:
   * Untrained input columns are a general hazard. train_dp.py:120-121 gives a constant input sd = 1, and because the input is always 0 its first-layer weights never receive a gradient. When RL, floors or other points later make that input vary, the frozen net gets an uncalibrated push. Here the input is 32 (team rat traps) at PRE and POST. zdb never places rat traps (placeRatTrap appears only at Baby.java:712, Baby.java:1980 and DK.java:614, all net-only paths). In cpi-x0 this input causes 90% of PRE fallbacks (THROW with nothing carried) and about 100 suppressed POST deliveries per game. Every point should be audited against its imitation data; constq.py does this for points 2-4. Fix by zeroing such columns at export and in widen (dpnet_io.py:215), or by clamping inputs to the imitation range.
   * Mask and executor disagree. For options 0-20 of points 2 and 3, actOk (Baby.java:2056-2057) offers every option whether or not its executor can act, and faceOk (2083-2084) does the same for FACE. Exactly-masked options (pass-3 21-38, F_DIR) never fall back. The floor of 0.005 is spread evenly over the offered options, about 150 picks per option per 650k records. rl_train.py uses the fallback flag only for diagnostics (rl_train.py:536-541), so the policy gradient credits a k that never ran. That is harmless while points 2-4 are frozen, but configs/cpi-x0.json lists phase_b_unlocks [[2,3,4],[6],[1]]. Before unlocking them, add exact masks, or relabel or drop fallback records.
   * Executors embed zdb's 'whether' logic: the THROW hold rule, cheese and budget floors, trap caps, coop-only lanes. The net decides 'whether' without seeing those inputs. The result is systematic fallback (THROW at carry age < 6 falls back 90% of the time), and RL cannot learn to override zdb's gates. Principle for the refactor: executors do the deterministic 'how' (target and geometry), gates become inputs, and the label stays zdb's decision.
   * Points are coupled through their inputs. Exploration on one point shifts the state distribution that every other point sees. In cpi-x0 the KING_ACT floor of 0.1 gave 51 A_TRAP_FOE/A_TRAP_FORT picks per game, against 6.6 in rl-m2; with the babies' X_RAT_TRAP_FWD floor picks, 91.6% of PRE rows had own rat traps, against 0% in imitation. Floors and unlock schedules should be judged by what they do to the frozen points' inputs. Backstab is the same kind of risk: actExec bites and grabs have no hostile() check, so a floor pick during coop flips coop for both teams for the rest of the game (InternalRobot.java:623-625, 643-651).
   * A_PASS bundles 'no action' with 'skip the rest of zdb's logic'. That logic includes the promotions at PRE (Baby.java:174-185) and tryDeliver, layLane and the bites at POST (209-226). Once masks are exact and promotions are explicit options, PASS should mean only 'no action'.
   * Data retention. cpi-x0's raw decision files were deleted after rl_data extraction (games/j*/ keeps only result.json and trace.tsv), and the npz has no label column, so what zdb did on fallback rows cannot be recovered. decisions.read_rl already returns 'label' (decisions.py:97-150); adding it to rl_data's output would make fallback analysis direct. rl-m2's per-record data is also not on the box, so the nRatT explanation of its 1.4% THROW rate is inferred, not measured.
