==================== map-baby-role-comms-mine-free

## 5 BABY_ROLE (6 options) kind: parameters: a persistent mode switch (the role, scout and raider flags) that zdb's cascade and every baby net read, plus R_KEEP, a do-nothing option offered only at reviews
-- fallback_explanation: Fallback is always 0 by construction, so it says nothing here. D.fallback flags k != label (D.java:159-162), but the BABY_ROLE label is computed after the net's option has run: at birth from roleLab() after roleExec (Baby.java:45-46), and at a review as KEEP if roleLab() is unchanged, otherwise roleLab() (88-89). roleSet cannot fail, so the label always equals k. What actually goes wrong is oscillation. In v0 there were 4,229 review flips, 0.54% of 788,318 reviews, where the floor alone predicts about 631. 81% of them are GATHER<->FIGHT (1,757 + 1,676). Of 3,469 consecutive flip pairs, 3,075 (88.6%) return to the role held before the flip. About 170 rats flip 15 or more times, which means every review for 150-180 rounds. The median round of a rat's first flip is 669. iter-0001 shows the same pattern (2,599 of 2,921 pairs return). To find the cause, I recomputed the v0 logits on 1,500 sampled flips and 1,500 keeps. 1,298 of the flips were the net's argmax, and in 1,164 of those (90%) the rat's current role scored above KEEP: by a median of +93 logits, with the new role at a median of +105. The cause is in the history of the code. Before pass 3 the review label was roleLab() with no mask (git show 0f50132, Baby.java hunk at about lines 86-89), so the imitation net learned to put a very large logit on the current role at reviews. Pass 3 masked that role out and added a constant KEEP. The rl-m2 start net was widened without calibration records (its 'widened' metadata has b_new -20 and r_keep_bias 60), so KEEP got the default 60 instead of the documented p99.9 of the old max logit plus 8 (docs/decision-points.md:191). The result: when the runner-up role's logit tops 60, the rat flips, and at the next review the role it left is now the runner-up, still above 60, so it flips back. Under RL the churn grew. At v38 (floor .001), non-KEEP picks are 35,188 of 665,035 rows including births; assuming births are about 3.1% of rows (the v0/v1 ratio), roughly 2.3% of reviews flip, and the upper bound is 5.3%. In cpi-x0 (floor .03) about 14% of reviews flip. Two separate estimates agree that about 12% of reviews are flips the net itself chose: the option histogram minus births minus the 2.4% floor share, and devfb's 217 deviations to KEEP divided by 0.006, about 36k of 300k records.
-- context_dependence: A role is a mode that lasts many turns, not an action. Its effect depends on the game phase (rush window, SCOUT_UNTIL, round parity), on the rat's slot, on what it knows about the enemy king and the mines, and on the downstream nets. Those nets were trained only on zdb's birth-time assignments: zdb sets the role once in born(), never changes it, and never sets raider. Every RL role change therefore puts all baby nets outside their training data. Each real change also resets navigation and exploration state, and attacker or raid state (Baby.java:1898-1905), so a flip costs something even when the new role is no better.
-- features: D.babyRole D.java:510-551, 112 inputs: 32 base inputs (ph; prev role, 3 at birth; rat cost; round/10; round % FIGHT_EVERY; rush window; slot scout bucket; ...; ekSeen at col 27), plus 16 comms-board and 64 raw shared-array columns. The last 80 were added with zero weights ('widened' cols_added 80) and are still near zero after 2 RL updates (|W1| 0.008-0.017 in v2). So team composition and heard information are effectively unused.
-- missing_information: The cpi-x0/v39 p5.npz is not available locally. I therefore cannot confirm that the roughly 12% of reviews where the net itself flips the role in cpi-x0 are the same back-and-forth flipping. Nor can I explain why the higher floor amplifies churn; one candidate is that entering ATTACK sets ekSeen, which feeds a heavily weighted input. The win-rate cost or benefit of a flip has not been measured.
-- generalization_proposal: 1. Fix how 'stay' is encoded. This is small and removes the measured flip-flopping. At reviews, offer all five roles and treat the current role as 'stay', so nothing is masked. This is exactly how the pass-2 net was trained, so its large current-role logits become the right signal again. Drop R_KEEP, or set z[KEEP] = max(z[KEEP], z[current]) before sampling. Note that roleExec's reset guard at Baby.java:1898 checks D.has(BABY_ROLE, R_KEEP) and would need a different condition. The minimal alternative is to re-widen point 5 with --calib so KEEP sits at p99.9+8, as documented. 2. Add commitment. A role change resets state, so apply the exploration floor only at birth or only on the stay option. Also add a minimum tenure after a change and/or review less often (Cfg.DP_ROLE_EVERY). 3. Make every option meaningful whenever it is offered: mask R_SCOUT from round SCOUT_UNTIL on, or drop that limit in net mode. 4. Role-diverse imitation, which closes the main capability gap. In the recording build, randomly reassign roles at birth and at some reviews, raider included. zdb's cascade takes the role as a parameter, so it yields correct labels for every role in every context. Then retrain the downstream baby nets so that role changes and raider=1 are covered by training data, which also trains BABY_MOVE's raider column. 5. Optional, for team composition: kings publish target role shares in a shared-array register, and team role counts become BABY_ROLE features. That lets the policy learn a team mix instead of each rat deciding on its own.
-- proposal_cost: Item 1: 1-2 h of Java plus a re-widen. No new data is needed, because existing review records relabel to the current role. Items 2-3: trivial. Item 4: 1-3 days (recording-build hook, new imitation games, retraining BABY_MOVE/ACT and the other baby nets, a parity re-check). Item 5: several days; it touches king comms.
-- coupling:
   * All baby nets and zdb's cascade read role, scout and raider: hostile() Baby.java:240-242; cascade gates at 1014, 1033-1035, 1040, 1059-1076, 1081, 1085; war() at 1249/1256/1268; pressKing at 1169/1171; netAllowed at 2276/2291-2293; netExec at 2366/2418/2421; netMineBlock at 2473-2480; features at D.java:247, 334, 462, 577, 834.
   * MINE_PICK: entering ATTACK clears mineTarget (Baby.java:1904), which forces a new pick. Role is also a MINE_PICK feature.
   * BABY_FREE: at ph 0, zdb's pickup is skipped for attackers (Baby.java:464).
   * BABY_MOVE: raider column 22 is untrained, so R_RAIDER pushes BABY_MOVE outside its training data.
   * Every real change resets navigation and exploration state (Baby.java:1898-1905), so a role flip-flop also stalls movement.

## 6 BABY_COMMS (15 options) kind: mixed: message types whose payload zdb's rules fix (primitive actions with hard-coded parameters), silence, a raw 32-bit escape (C_RAW, with the digits chosen by BABY_VAL), and a relay of a heard net-type squeak
-- fallback_explanation: This is the only point among 5-9 where fallback is real. If commsExec fails, the label stays zdb's, as set by the muted cashTick/report (Baby.java:160-161), and zdb's held squeak goes out (164-165), which D.fallback flags. The mask is static: only C_RAW and C_RELAY depend on state (2088-2096), while every message executor has a precondition. So options picked through the floor mostly fail. In cpi-x0 the fallback rates are: SYM_GOSSIP .973, SYM_KING .817, CASH_RELAY .662, CASH_LEAD .380, MINE_GOSSIP .144, CAT .115, MINE_KING .066, MINE_RELAY .015. C_RAW's 0 is structural: its record is closed before its digits are drawn.
-- context_dependence: The net chooses only the message type. The payload is fixed by zdb's rules: which mine (myMines[round % count], the first unreported or unlisted mine), Sym.mask, the cat that was seen, cashSpot(G.me). zdb sends king-directed messages only within dist2 16 of a king and gossip or relays only away from one (Baby.java:904-930). The executors drop that proximity condition. Squeaks are heard by friends, enemies and cats, and cats turn toward squeaks (docs/rules/specs.txt:569-617). This hidden cost is not in the features. The net's comms-board inputs are zero (cols 37-40 and 47-48 measured 0.0 in v0 and v2), and the point is frozen, so what a rat has heard never affects what it sends.
-- features: D.babyComms D.java:571-636: 37 base inputs computed before cashTick/report, plus 16 comms-board columns that widen added with zero weights and that are still zero (frozen in rl-m2 and cpi-x0).
-- missing_information: How often each type's precondition holds is not recorded. Whether aliased raw squeaks ever reached a king and corrupted symmetry would need king-side logs. cpi-x0 point 6 is only a 5% sample, and devfb leaves out secondary-slot records.
-- generalization_proposal: 1. Make the mask equal executability. Compute commsOk from the executors' own predicates (myMineCount>0, unreported()>=0, symNews(), unlisted()>=0, catNews(), a cash site or cashSpot within CASH_PULL) after the muted zdb pass, so the mask sees the same state the executor will. This stops wasting floor picks and makes fallback 0 by construction. 2. Remove dead and duplicate options: C_SYM_GOSSIP duplicates C_SYM_KING; C_EKING is always masked although it has an executor; options 10-12 have no executor. Either implement them (enemy-king and trap sightings are useful) or drop them. 3. Score (type, payload) pairs. This addresses the real constraint. Score each candidate (every known own, heard or unreported mine, the symmetry, each seen cat, the enemy king, candidate cash sites) with a shared per-candidate scorer that includes a 'zdb would send this' indicator feature. Imitation stays exact and RL is free to deviate. 4. Reserve a net-only type nibble for C_RAW, and for KING_COMMS raw (DK.java:858), so a raw word can never parse as a zdb message. 5. Train the listeners: give the receiving points decoded heard-message features with nonzero, trainable weights. Otherwise sender choices cannot earn reward beyond what zdb's own decoders do with them.
-- proposal_cost: Items 1-2: small Java changes; the frozen net is unchanged. Item 4: small, but touches both decoders. Item 3: medium to large (variable candidate list, new features, re-imitation through the indicator). Item 5: needs re-imitation, or RL with the listener points trainable.
-- coupling:
   * BABY_VAL (point 9): C_RAW opens 7 digit records.
   * Receivers act on any squeak whose type is a zdb type (King.java:131-151). SQ_MINE -> Comms.addMine, so a bogus or off-map mine. SQ_SYM -> Sym.merge(extra&7) (Sym.java:50-54), which can eliminate the true symmetry and is published by King.java:166-175. SQ_EKING overwrites the enemy-king slots. SQ_CAT -> noteCat. Babies record SQ_MINE through hearMines (Baby.java:934-939).
   * zdb's muted cash and report flow runs as if its squeak went out (Baby.java:941-942). So CASH_* executors see zdb's cash state, not the result of what the net actually sent.
   * C_RELAY only does something when other rats send C_RAW, which happens only through the floor today.

## 7 MINE_PICK (40 options) kind: candidate targets: option i is the i-th entry of pickMine's sorted candidate list. It is a position in that list, not a fixed mine.
-- fallback_explanation: Fallback is structurally 0: the executor writes label = k and always returns cand[k]. The informative measure is fidelity to zdb's rule, cand[(slot % m + mineShift) % m], whose two inputs are themselves features (D.java:831-832). The v0 imitation net matches it on 81.7% of 115,006 picks: 100% at m=1, at least 98% at m<=3, 91-95% at m=4-5, 70% at m=8, 47% at m=11, and 19.8% at m>=12. The commonest miss is off by one: 6,854 of 21,007 misses. An MLP cannot represent this modular rotation for larger m, so the RL start already does not reproduce zdb's mine allocation wherever many mines are known. In v2 the heaviest inputs are slot%m (0.94) and shift%m (0.83), against a median of 0.16.
-- context_dependence: Option indices are positions whose mine changes across calls and rats. Whenever a home-half mine is known, only home-half mines are offered (m = ours). In 80.3% of v0 picks, a mean of 3.6 known enemy-half mines could not be chosen. Each candidate's features are only its distance from the rat and from home, kingHeld, and allies within 4 among robots currently in sight, and only for the first 8. There is no per-mine yield, crowding beyond sight, enemy presence, or visit history. The pick is sticky, so its consequences last many turns while the decision sees only the current instant.
-- features: 45 inputs (D.java:824-852): m, n, ours, slot%m, mineShift%m, pickSite, role, coop, round/10, numMines, myMineCount, numKings, raw cheese, then 8 x (distance from rat, distance from home, kingHeld, allies within 4), padded with 63/63/0/0.
-- missing_information: The cpi-x0/v39 p7 data is not available locally, so the trained net's fidelity is unknown. Per-mine productivity and crowding are not logged, so whether better mine choice matters has not been measured.
-- generalization_proposal: 1. Score each candidate with a shared network (pointer-style): logit_i = g(features of candidate i, global features), with weights shared across candidates, for every candidate up to 40. The 40-logit output, the mineOk mask, pick2 and the data format stay as they are. In DNet this can be a weight-shared first layer. 2. Per-candidate features: distance from the rat and from home, a home-half flag, kingHeld, allies and enemies near the mine (sensed or reported), the time since the rat last visited it and its stall count there (rat memory), cheese last seen there, its rank, and an indicator 'zdb's rotation picks this one'. With that indicator, imitation is exact (one feature decides the pick), and RL can learn from the other features when to override it. 3. Offer every known mine. Keep home half as a feature, so contesting or raiding enemy-half mines becomes possible. 4. Optional: a 'drop the assignment / explore' option, instead of relying only on the stall>25 timer.
-- proposal_cost: Medium, about 1-2 days. It needs a per-candidate Java feature block (bytecode limits are currently off per project memory), a shared-scorer forward pass in DNet, support for a shared-weight point in train_dp and rl_train (or a candidate-major MLP), and re-imitation, which is easy because the indicator gives the labels.
-- coupling:
   * BABY_ROLE: switching to ATTACK clears mineTarget, and role is a MINE_PICK feature (D.java:834).
   * BABY_MOVE: netMineBlock Baby.java:2470-2489 uses mineTarget, and M_MINE_EXPLORE clears it (2416).
   * BABY_COMMS decides which mines teammates learn about, and those mines are the candidate list.
   * shiftMine increments mineShift after idling (1128-1140), which re-picks with a rotated rule.

## 8 BABY_FREE (16 options) kind: mixed: FR_ZDB, which runs zdb's own pickup; pickup policies (take all, take none, caps of 10/25/50/100/200 for this turn); and trap removals indexed by target (own rat or cat trap on the rat's tile or fan tiles 0..2), chained up to FREE_CHAIN=2
-- fallback_explanation: Fallback is structurally 0. Options 1-7 only set the turn's pickCap, FR_ZDB runs zdb's code, and a failed removal drops its record instead of flagging it (Baby.java:456). The net is also constant. widen's 'calibrated' init gives zero last-layer weights, bias 0 on FR_ZDB and -10.37 on every other option (identical in rl-m2 v0 and v2; point 8 is not trainable). So the choice ignores all features, and every non-ZDB pick is a floor pick: dev_rate .0027, about .005 x (1 - 1/2.28 mean allowed).
-- context_dependence: FR_ZDB means 'whatever zdb would do' (take all, take none, or skip for an attacker). The explicit option equal to zdb's choice is masked out (FR_ALL is offered only when zdb takes nothing, FR_NONE only when zdb takes something). This is the same pattern as R_KEEP: a shortcut to the default action plus a hidden twin. Caps are offered only when they would bind, so their meaning depends on the cheese carried and reachable. Carried raw cheese slows movement. Trap removal has no cooldown and no refund (docs/rules/engine-checked-digest.md:150).
-- features: D.babyFree D.java:726-769, 36 inputs per the 'widened' metadata. Unused, because the net is constant.
-- missing_information: How often zdb's pickup choice or a trap removal costs value has not been measured. The point is frozen, and only 5% of its records are sampled.
-- generalization_proposal: 1. Replace the FR_ZDB shortcut with explicit actions: label zdb's choice as the explicit option it equals (ALL, NONE, or the attacker skip), stop masking that twin, and add a 'zdb's choice' indicator feature. 2. Treat the cap as an ordered parameter: always offer the fixed ladder, clipped to what is reachable. 3. Trap removal: add features (trap type and position, enemies and cats nearby, own trap count) and use a lower floor, or make it a separate point. 4. Make the point trainable, initialised from the indicator feature rather than a constant bias, so its features matter at all.
-- proposal_cost: Small to medium: Java mask and label changes plus re-imitation. Labels already exist: 100,771 FR_ZDB records per 24 recording games (docs/decision-points.md:205). Trap features: about 1 day.
-- coupling:
   * pickCap lasts the whole turn and caps later pickups, including pickAdjacent (Baby.java:40, 433-445).
   * Cheese carried slows BABY_MOVE and changes when the rat deposits.
   * BABY_ROLE: at ph 0 the pickup is skipped for attackers (Baby.java:464).

## 9 BABY_VAL (32 options) kind: parameters: one base-32 digit of a raw 32-bit squeak payload, with no meaning defined on either the sending or the receiving side
-- fallback_explanation: Fallback is structurally 0. A digit record cannot fail. A failed draw (D.dfail) or squeak only makes the C_RAW squeak fail, and the COMMS record was already closed with label C_RAW (Baby.java:2169-2170).
-- context_dependence: The net is uniform: widen gives value points all-zero last layers and biases (docs/decision-points.md:194; rl-m2 v0 and v2 identical). There are no imitation labels. So raw squeaks are random words. Receivers parse every squeak with zdb's decoder, and a uniform word's type nibble is a zdb type (types 1-4 and SQ_CASH, D.java:645) with probability 5/16. That makes about 8 of the roughly 26 raw squeaks per cpi-x0 game look like zdb messages to any king or rat that hears them.
-- features: D.babyVal D.java:773-796, 30 inputs per the 'widened' metadata: purpose, digit position, the prefix so far. Unused, because the net is uniform.
-- missing_information: Whether any aliased word reached a king in cpi-x0 is unknown; there is no king-side logging. The receiver-side design has not been decided.
-- generalization_proposal: 1. Reserve a net-only type nibble, plus a length or purpose tag. 2. Replace free digits with typed fields: a small net vocabulary of message types, and payloads chosen by pointing at candidates (a known mine, cat, the enemy king, the rat's own position, a small enum). This is the same per-candidate scorer as proposed for BABY_COMMS. 3. Bootstrap by encoding zdb's own squeaks in the field format as imitation labels, so the raw channel can at least reproduce zdb's protocol and RL has a working protocol to modify. 4. Train senders and receivers together: give the receiving points decoded net-type messages as trainable features. Otherwise no reward signal can reach the sender.
-- proposal_cost: Medium to large: protocol design, two decoders, receiver features, and RL across several points.
-- coupling:
   * Reachable only through BABY_COMMS C_RAW and KING_COMMS raw.
   * C_RELAY re-squeaks the newest net-type word heard (Baby.java:2174-2176).
   * Kings and babies act on words that look like zdb messages (King.java:131-151, Baby.java:934-939).

-- cross_cutting:
   * A fallback rate of 0 says nothing about feasibility at points 5, 7, 8 and 9. Their labels are written after the net's option runs (Baby.java:45-46 and 88-89; minePickExec 2185; C_RAW 2169), or a failed step drops its record (Baby.java:456). Only BABY_COMMS reports real failures (Baby.java:164-165). The 'all fallback 0' rows in cpi-x0 for 5/7/8/9 are therefore not evidence that their action spaces fit.
   * The same encoding problem recurs: a shortcut option for the default action, with its explicit twin masked out. R_KEEP is offered while the current role is masked (D.roleOk D.java:555-561); FR_ZDB is offered while FR_ALL/FR_NONE are masked when they equal zdb's choice (freeOk Baby.java:470-489). One behaviour gets two logits whose relationship the net never learned. At BABY_ROLE this is a measured failure. The pass-2 net put its large review logit on the current role, because the old label was roleLab() with no mask (git show 0f50132). Pass 3 masked that role out and added a constant KEEP of 60, uncalibrated: the start net's 'widened' metadata has r_keep_bias 60 and b_new -20, against the p99.9+8 documented at docs/decision-points.md:191. In v0, 90% of the argmax flips I resampled had current-role logit > KEEP, and 88.6% of consecutive flips return to the previous role: GATHER<->FIGHT flipping, with about 170 rats flipping at every review. Rule: express 'keep' or 'zdb's choice' as an indicator feature on the explicit option, never as a separate option with its twin masked.
   * Options that are list positions or shortcuts mean different things in different contexts (MINE_PICK ranks, FR_ZDB, C_RELAY, BABY_VAL digits), and fixed-size MLP heads cannot learn them. The imitation start reproduces zdb's own MINE_PICK rule on only 81.7% of picks, and on 19.8% at m>=12. A shared per-candidate scorer with a 'zdb's choice' indicator generalises across list sizes, makes imitation exact, and keeps the existing 40-logit and mask plumbing.
   * Hard-coded restrictions shrink the reachable space more than the option counts suggest. MINE_PICK never offers enemy-half mines while any home-half mine is known (80.3% of v0 picks, a mean of 3.6 mines excluded). BABY_COMMS payloads are fixed by zdb's rules, 4 of its 15 options are always masked (8, 10-12), and option 9 duplicates option 3. R_SCOUT does nothing after round 150. R_RAIDER and review-time role changes have no imitation data at all.
   * Untrained inputs put new actions outside the downstream nets' training data. Columns that widen added are zero in frozen nets: BABY_MOVE's comms block (cols 60-75 measured 0), BABY_COMMS's 16 comms-board columns, and BABY_ROLE's 80 new columns, which are still about 0 after 2 RL updates. Columns that were constant during imitation keep their default init: BABY_MOVE raider col 22 reads 0.0615, against about 0.065 expected for its init, while the trained scout col 23 reads 0.129. So R_RAIDER, role flips and any comms content are either ignored or met with untrained responses. Meeting the capability-complete principle needs role-diverse and comms-diverse imitation data, not just more options.
   * Uniform exploration floors on decisions that last many turns have long-lasting effects. A floor pick at BABY_ROLE (and at MINE_PICK) persists for many turns and resets navigation state (Baby.java:1898-1905). With cpi-x0's ROLE floor of .03, about 14% of reviews flipped, about 12% chosen by the net itself (two estimates agree). That compares with at most 5.3% at v38 (floor .001) and 0.54% at v0. Exploration on mode switches belongs on the stay option or at birth only, with a minimum tenure.
   * A mask should equal what the executor can actually do. BABY_COMMS's static mask wastes floor exploration (38-97% fallback on options 3, 6, 7 and 9) and makes its fallback signal noisy. The mask should be computed from the executors' own predicates after the muted zdb pass.
   * Communication needs both a sender and a listener. Sender options (BABY_COMMS types, C_RAW/BABY_VAL) cannot become meaningful while receivers' message inputs are zero or frozen, and while raw words have a 5/16 chance of parsing as zdb messages (kings then add bogus mines or merge symmetry bits: King.java:131-151, Sym.java:50-54). Train or un-zero the receivers and reserve a net-only namespace before widening the sender action space.
   * Diagnostics have blind spots. Secondary-slot points (6, 7, 9) are absent from devfb, which keeps primary records only. Frozen points (6, 8, 9) are sampled at 5% (scripts/rl_data.py:148). The BABY_ROLE back-and-forth flipping does not show up in option histograms. Per-robot transition statistics, as computed by /tmp/claude-1000/-home-terryvanbelle-projects-vibe-bc26a0/31310128-fb77-4058-8e53-1da41e55d47b/scratchpad/aspace5/p5trans.py, should become a standard check for points whose effect persists.

## 10 KING_MOVE (37 options) kind: Mixed. 20 options are labels for the exits of zdb's King.move cascade (DK.java:25-29): 4 are masked dead labels, 7 are stays that behave identically, 1 (K_CAT_FLEE) is zdb's own first branch, and 8 are conditional movement or dig executors (dodge, form-still, unplug, pile, walk-home, closer, sidestep, dig). The other 17 are net-only: an explicit K_STAY, 8 plain steps (K_MOVE_N) and 8 dirt-step digs (K_DIGSTEP_N). zdb never labels any of these 17.
-- fallback_explanation: Whenever the king is movement-ready, the static mask offers the same 33 options (DK.java:350-355; the data shows 33.0 allowed options in every row). 25 of those options have executor preconditions that the mask never checks. When the net picks one that cannot act, netKingMove returns false and zdb's whole cascade runs instead (King.java:79, 378-401).

In the probe, 105,560 of 602,744 records (17.5%) fell back. The 8 stays never fail (448,606 picks, 74.4%), so 68.5% of the 154,138 non-stay picks fell back.

Share of all fallbacks by option:
- K_CLOSER 36.9%: cannot act at home, because the executor needs the home mine more than 1 tile away (DK.java:403); otherwise fails when no fan3 step is free, shortens dist2 and is unplugged (King.java:608-618).
- K_PILE 17.7%: no pile within the leash, or no fan5 step.
- K_DIGSTEP_N 13.7%: dirtStep is null, or the action is not ready.
- K_MOVE_N 9.0%: canMove is false.
- K_SIDESTEP 8.3%.
- K_UNPLUG 3.8%: needs ringArcs of 2 or more, which holds in only 4.3% of rows.
- K_DIG 3.1%.
- K_CAT_FLEE 2.6%.
- K_FORM_STILL 1.9%: always fails for kings other than slot 0.
- K_DODGE 1.7%.
- K_WALK_HOME 1.3%.

The net cannot see most of these preconditions:
- canMove and dirtStep arrive as two packed 8-bit integers (DK.java:126-132).
- 'At home' is only a capped Chebyshev distance (DK.java:140).
- No feature says whether a fan3 or fan5 step toward home or a pile is free and unplugged.

Even the imitation anchor (runs/m2/anchor) puts K_CLOSER as argmax in 16,337 at-home rows, where it can never act. Its errors at this point are invisible because the fallback runs zdb. Deviations fall back 57.8% of the time, and top choices 9.05%.

Why this matters for learning: D.fallback only sets a flag (D.java:158-162), and rl_train trains on every record whose k is not 'none' (scripts/rl_train.py:525; the flag is only a diagnostic, 536-541). So 17.5% of records give credit for whatever zdb did to an option that never acted.
-- context_dependence: Each of zdb's labels implies that every earlier branch failed (King.java:382-400). For example, K_AT_HOME means: no flee, no enemy or cat in view, no unplug step, no pile step, home known, and within 1 tile of it. K_SIDESTEP further means: no closer step, action ready, cheese at least 100, no dig tile (King.java:393-398).

The net-mode executors drop those conditions (DK.java:382-433):
- K_PILE steps toward a pile even with enemies in view.
- K_CLOSER ignores a nearer pile.
- K_SIDESTEP needs no action and no cheese.
- K_DIG skips the KING_DIG_MIN guard (DK.java:444-449).
So an option is 'what zdb did' in the states it was imitated on, and a different deviation everywhere else.

## 12 KING_ACT (17 options) kind: Mixed. Option 0 is hold. Options 1-10 are typed primitive actions, each with a hard-coded target rule: bite the weakest (kill or plain), the nearest or a king's centre; bite a cat; rat trap toward a foe or the centre; cat trap; dirt fill; dig. Options 11-16 are parameters: bite cheese 1/4/9/16/36/100 on the weakest target. zdb itself only ever produces A_BITE_KILL, A_BITE or A_NONE (King.java:196-197, 70).
-- fallback_explanation: actOk masks only the bite ladder (11-16). Options 1-10 are offered on every action-ready turn (DK.java:594), although most need a target that is rarely there: an enemy in bite reach (0.3% of records), a cat tile in reach (0.6%), an enemy baby in sight (TRAP_FOE), the coop/backstab window (CAT_TRAP), or removable ring dirt (20.1% of records) plus bank >= 45 (DIG). A failing pick returns false or throws (caught at DK.java:499). The label stays unset and zdb's bite runs (King.java:57), which is itself a no-op without a target, so the record is flagged as a fallback and plays as A_NONE. Fallback matches the recorded precondition features exactly for option 5 (nt==0) and option 10 (nr==0 or bank < 45), and nearly exactly for the bites (reach==0). Apart from A_NONE, only TRAP_FORT and MOAT_FILL usually execute. Fallback rate over all records: 7.71% in cpi-x0, 1.19% in rl-m2 v38. The plan's table says 79% of deviations fall back (docs/rl-es-plan.md:500-506).
-- context_dependence: Each option fixes its target (weakest, nearest, king centre, map centre, nearest cat), so the same output index acts on different tiles in different positions. Most options are no-ops outside rare situations (combat in reach, cats, coop). The two that usually execute, fort trap and moat fill, have effects outside this point: they use the ring tiles KING_SPAWN builds on, they put the king's action on cooldown for 1-2 turns (which closes KING_SPAWN next turn, King.java:289), and rat traps count toward the babies' TRAP_CAP. The executors do not check G.coop, while zdb's bite does (King.java:57). In cooperation, a bite on an enemy rat would trigger the engine's backstab rule. No such bite was executed in the data.
-- features: 38 inputs (DK.java:219-289): coop, round/10, health, bank, enemy babies and kings in sight, threat count, nearest-baby d2, reach count, weakest target's health and whether it is a king, kill-bite possible, king in reach, cats, cat distance, catReach, team rat traps, TRAP_CAP, counts of ring tiles where a rat trap or dirt can be placed or dirt removed (counts, not per tile), team dirt, moat flag (always false, DK.java:279), spawnWhy, spawned, numKings, x, y, W, H, and 8 kmini values. Missing: surplus and income, cat-trap count, per-tile and per-target detail, and the coop/backstab window.
-- missing_information: I did not measure the game value of fort traps or moat dirt; sens12 mixes all options. I did not check whether a trap on a ring tile blocks canBuildRat, or what supplies team dirt (np > 0 in 89.2% of records).
-- generalization_proposal: Use typed actions, each with its own targets and a precondition mask. The options become: none; bite target j with cheese c, scored per target over the enemies and cats in reach (type, health, whether c kills, distance, is a king), with a small cheese-ladder head masked by canAttack(at, c); and rat trap, cat trap, place dirt or remove dirt at ring tile t, using one shared per-tile scorer with a type embedding and per-tile masks from canPlaceRatTrap, canPlaceCatTrap, canPlaceDirt and canRemoveDirt. Best of all, merge this point with KING_SPAWN into one decision per action-ready turn (hold / build at t / bite j / trap at t / dirt at t / dig at t), because the two compete for the same action, tiles and bank. Imitation would reproduce zdb's spawn-first order. Floor 0.2 here scored 0.4375 [0.32, 0.56] against v39 (runs/cpi/sens12).

## 13 KING_FORM (30 options) kind: Mixed. Options 0-12 are labels named after zdb's formation branches. With no site open, 0-2 and 8-12 all do nothing. With a site open, 0-6 all close it (4 also resets the retry timer) and 7 holds it. Options 13-17 are five site heuristics (mine, back, ring, crowd, mine-site). Options 18-29 are candidate targets: the 12 RING sites at distance 3, oriented by map half.
-- fallback_explanation: formAllowed masks options by whether a site is open and whether a king slot is free, so labels 0-12 never fall back. The openers fall back when their site helper returns null or the site fails its clearance test: F_RING_i when RING[i] fails clearNow (100% match with the rm bit), RING and CROWD when no ring site is clear (rm==0), MINE when pickFormSite returns the back spot (kind != 1), and BACK when the 3x3 four tiles back fails clear3. After a fallback, zdb's formation() runs (King.java:74), which mostly closes the site or idles. Fallback rate over all records: 4.18% in cpi-x0, 0.42% in rl-m2 v38. The plan's table says 37% of deviations fall back (docs/rl-es-plan.md:500-506).
-- context_dependence: Opening a site is a plan that spans many turns, but this point re-decides it every turn. Babies join only while FORM_T is fresh (2 rounds, Baby.java:1530) and only from within r2 72 (Baby.java:1539). A king forms only when babies fill the 3x3 and the one on the centre can becomeRatKing (Baby.java:1535-1557). The net has to pick F_HOLD again each turn against its imitation prior, and that prior closes the site: in cpi-x0 a net-opened site stays open a median of 1 more record, and there were 24.2 opens per team-game (opened from zdb states WAR_OFF 4,292, CAP_OFF 4,362, IDLE 3,597, gate-pass 140). With no site open, 8 of the 25 allowed options do the same nothing and 17 are opens, so 68% of a uniform floor means 'open now'. RING sites are mirrored by map half (DK.java:678-679), and the heuristic openers depend on mine knowledge and symmetry. The openers skip all of zdb's gates (FORM_FROM 80, TTL 60 + RETRY 150, 16 babies, 300 cheese, war < 300, FORM_MAX 2, rich, income; Cfg.java:44-46, 212). Outcome in 1-king states: a new king appeared within 15 turns after 381 of 7,235 net opens (5.3%), against 1.8% for random 1-king records with no site, and 22 of 150 (14.7%) for opens where zdb's gate feature 15 passed. In rl-m2 v38: 3.7 opens per team-game, and 63 of 591 (10.7%) against a 0.8% baseline.
-- features: 38 inputs (DK.java:292-342): coop, round, numKings, site open, rounds since formStart, formKings, babies, bank, income rate, surplus, war-off flag, cap reached, rich0, endKing, formIncome, zdb's FORM_FROM/TTL+RETRY/babies/cheese gate, distance to the site, king on the site, pickFormSite kind and distance, rm (clearNow bitmask of the 12 RING sites, a raw integer), distance to the enemy start, numMines, symmetry bits, sawEnemy, own babies in sight, x, y, W, H, and 8 kmini values. Missing: babies near each candidate site, per-site geometry beyond the bits of rm, and the mine-site candidates.
-- missing_information: 'New king within 15 turns' is a proxy: nothing is attributed, and only slot-0 records are used. I did not measure what babies pulled to a one-turn site lose (mining, fighting), or how often KING_COMMS edits the FORM slots. Records do not store which site an opener chose.
-- generalization_proposal: Restructure the options around candidates: keep; close (offered only while a site is open); and open at candidate c (offered only while no site is open and a slot is free). One shared per-candidate scorer covers all candidate sites: the 12 RING sites, the mines that pickFormSite and mineSite consider, the back spot and the crowd site, deduplicated. A single exploratory open then has its real consequence instead of a one-turn blip. zdb's gates become input features (most already are: 10-15). -- cross_cutting:
   * Fallen-back picks still drive the RL gradient. rl_train keeps every record whose k is allowed by the recorded mask (scripts/rl_train.py:525-535) and uses the fallback flag only for diagnostics (536-542). So picks that never executed (79% of deviations at KING_ACT, 39% at KING_SPAWN, 37% at KING_FORM; docs/rl-es-plan.md:500-506) get credit or blame for an outcome zdb's cascade produced. The fix belongs in the action space: masks that equal each executor's preconditions, so fallback is about 0. actOk (DK.java:592-598) is the only partial example today. Records with only one feasible option then drop out of the loss (rl_train.py:534-535).
   * Several labels do the same thing in the game. KING_SPAWN options 0/1/2/20/21 are identical; so are KING_FORM 0-2 and 8-12 with no site open, and 0-6 with a site open. The cpi-x0 net splits mass among them by whichever condition is salient (for example, 168k S_CAP picks in floor states). This gives RL flat directions and tilts uniform-floor exploration toward acting: 16 of 22 options are 'build' at KING_SPAWN, 17 of 25 are 'open' at KING_FORM, and 10 of 11 are 'act' at KING_ACT. Collapse to one label per distinct effect, and keep zdb's reason as a feature or an auxiliary imitation target.
   * At these points, floor exploration is permanent and is mostly 'act more'. At floor 0.1 (cpi-x0), per team-game, the net spawned 49 more rats than zdb would, opened 24 sites and placed about 27 traps, dirt tiles and digs. Floor 0.2 at a single point against v39 (64 games each, runs/cpi/sens<p> on the box): KING_SPAWN 0.266 [0.173, 0.385], KING_FORM 0.375, KING_ACT 0.4375, KING_MOVE 0.531. Exploration should be per meaningful choice (spawn vs hold), small, and masked.
   * The per-point nets cannot see the resources these points share. The king has one action per turn: spawn first, then KING_ACT only if still ready (King.java:50-54), with cooldowns of 10-25 (RobotControllerImpl.java:1016, 309-311; TrapType.java:12, 17). The 16 ring tiles serve builds, traps, dirt and digs (King.java:506-507; DK.java:606-614; Moat.java:45-55). The bank and the rat-cost escalation are shared, and so is the team trap count against the babies' TRAP_CAP (Baby.java:706). Recommendation: one king-action decision (hold / build at t / bite j / trap at t / dirt at t / dig at t) with per-candidate scoring, plus input features for the shared state.
   * Labels leak into other points' inputs. King.spawnWhy is set by the KING_SPAWN net's label (DK.java:466-478) and read by KING_MOVE (DK.java:150) and KING_ACT (DK.java:280). Features like this should come from the state (zdb's cascade predicate), not from the chosen label.
   * Bitmask features cannot be used as fed. KING_SPAWN's bm/dm and KING_FORM's rm are raw scalars, with normalisation folded into layer 1 (DNet.java:4-6, 61-66). An MLP cannot read bit i of a 16-bit integer. This fits the 100% match between fallback and a clear bit, and the 29-44% build fallback still present in rl-m2 v38. Use masks plus per-candidate inputs instead.
   * Choices that play out over many turns are re-decided every turn. Formation site lifetime and the spending schedule are both chosen by an imitation-trained argmax, which undoes an exploratory deviation on the next turn (a net-opened site lasts a median of 1 more record). Use commitments, such as a TTL held by the executor, or parameter heads (bank reserve, TTL, minimum babies) so that one decision has a lasting effect RL can learn from.
   * All three points need the same infrastructure. DNet needs a mode that scores a variable list of candidates with one small MLP (masked per-candidate logits), plus factored heads (type, then target). Records are sent as indicator strings (D.java:179), capped at 256 chars (GameConstants.java:53; truncation at RobotControllerImpl.java:1579-1581). Per-candidate inputs fit for each point alone (about 137-190 chars), but a merged KING_SPAWN+KING_ACT record would reach the limit. dpnet_io.probs (scripts/dpnet_io.py:110), build_dpnet, train_dp and rl_train need the new layouts. Then fresh zdb data with the new features, label conversion, and a greedy-parity check against zdb before RL.
